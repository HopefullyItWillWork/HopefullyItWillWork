Transaction history

Every roster move, newest first. The commissioner can reverse an entry.

Cap rules

Salary cap

The cap is soft. You can exceed it only through Bird rights, Early Bird, the mid-level exception, or minimum contracts.

Luxury tax

The tax line is a hard cap. No team's salary may exceed it at any point, for any reason. No exception beats it.

Bird rights

Three seasons with one club without clearing waivers or changing teams as a free agent. Lets the club exceed the cap to re-sign its own player, up to four years with raises to 7.5%. Rights travel in a trade.

Early Bird

Signed mid-season before the deadline and finished the year on the roster. Lets the club go up to $7.00 over the cap to re-sign him.

Restricted free agents

Only players who finished the last year of a team, player, or rookie option. The old club sits out the bidding, then chooses whether to match. All cap rules apply to the match.

Mid-level exception

Once a year. Over the cap $5.50 up to 4 years; under the cap $3.25 up to 2 years. May be split between players.

Minimum contracts

$1.00 for up to 2 years. Signable over the cap, with no limit on how many, as long as the hard cap holds.

Escalation

$0.75–$3.75 does not escalate. From $4.00 up, raises are 4.5% normally or 7.5% with Bird rights. Raises never compound and always round up to the next $0.25.

Cutting a player

Release anyone at any time. Cut in season and the salary stays on the cap until that season ends, then clears. Cut in the offseason and the money comes off immediately and every remaining year is voided.

On a deal of two years or more, the club that released him cannot re-sign him during the first offseason after the release. He may be signed during the following season to a one-year minimum contract; a multi-year deal has to wait for the next offseason. Other clubs face no restriction.

Trades

Over the cap, incoming salary is capped at 150% of outgoing up to $9.75, outgoing plus $5.00 from $10.00–$19.50, and 125% at $19.75 and up. Offseason mismatches create a trade exception that expires after the next deadline.

Rookie draft

Three years with a rookie option on the last. First pick is 3.57% of the cap rounded up to $0.25, each later pick $0.25 less. Rookies sign after the auction and don't eat auction cap space, though the hard cap still binds — a club with no room passes. One pick per club, in reverse order of finish. Anyone undrafted becomes an ordinary free agent and turns up in the auction.

Draft picks in trades

A club may trade this year's pick or one from a later draft. A future pick can carry a protection — "top N protected" keeps it at home if it lands in the first N slots — and the obligation can be set to roll to the next draft instead of lapsing. Picks move $0 and take up no roster spot.

Waivers

Claim order runs worst record to best, reset weekly. A claimed player finishes the season on a minimum contract, then becomes a free agent.

League settings

Clubs

Add a club to the league. It starts empty, with no PIN — the first person to sign in as it claims it. Nine clubs is the league as it stands; the cap, the tax and the 920-game limit are all per club, so nothing else needs changing.

Removing a club is permanent. Its roster, its release history, its picks and any offer it is part of go with it. The one case you are likely to need it for is a club that appears twice under an old and a new name — remove the stale one.

GM access

Set or reset each GM's PIN, and hold the address the league emails. A club with no PIN is claimed by whoever signs in first, so set them all before you share the link.

Email addresses are stored with the rosters. Like the PINs, anyone who can reach the league database can read them. Use an address you are comfortable sharing with the league, not one tied to anything else.

Commissioner access

A club named here keeps its own team — its own roster, its own notes, its own turn on the auction's nomination snake — and also gets this tab and everything on it, including awarding players at auction. It is a second commissioner who still has a club to run, not a second commissioner login. Only the commissioner login can change this list, so a deputy can neither promote himself nor remove the man who appointed him.

Season phase

The offseason allows auctions, cuts that clear salary immediately, and roster building. Once the season is live, cuts keep their salary on the books until the year ends, and the injured reserve and nightly lineups open up. Roster size is the ACTIVE limit — the injured reserve sits outside it, so 15 active with 1 IR slot is the rulebook's sixteen men, and only while one of them is hurt. Closing the season empties the injured reserve, which can leave a club one over; it is told, and its GM chooses who goes.

Advance the league year

Run this after a season has been played and before the next auction: it moves the league on to the following contract year. Every contract advances a year, deals with nothing owed in the new season become expiring, release bars from the season just gone lapse, and another season counts towards Bird rights. Nothing is rewritten to do it — a contract records what it owes in each named season, so advancing the year is all there is to it, and running it twice by accident cannot age anybody twice.

Auction nomination order

Round one runs top to bottom; round two runs back up, and so on — a snake. Only the club on the clock may put a player on the block, and a club already carrying a full roster is skipped, since it has nothing to bid with. Leave every row blank and nomination is a free-for-all, which is how it worked before.

Tip-off times

A player is frozen the moment his own game tips off — a five o'clock game locks that man at five and leaves his team-mate on a half-seven game free for another two and a half hours. These arrive on their own from the NBA's published schedule, once a day, and nothing below needs saving for the lock to work. The box is only an override: put a club here to correct a time the feed has wrong, or to run a night when the feed is down. One club per line, DEN 19:00, on a 24-hour clock in league time. Anything you enter beats the feed for that club and nothing else.

Rookie draft input

The order and the money are settled before anyone is on the clock, so both are entered here. Set a club against each slot — no. 1 is the club that finished last — and a salary for each. Auto-fill uses the rulebook scale: 3.57% of the cap for the first pick, rounded up to the next quarter, then a quarter less each pick. "Future drafts" is how many later drafts clubs may trade picks in.

Every player

Every contract in the league — the club, what he is owed, how long for, and the rights that club holds — plus every unsigned free agent in the player pool. Editing here writes straight to the roster and is logged like any other move. Unlike the Move tool above, expiring players are included, so a club's Bird or restricted rights can be corrected without signing anybody. An unrostered player has no contract to open, so his row opens the same dialog empty: give him a club and a salary and he is assigned, or leave the club blank and only his player record is saved — position, and the spelling the league spreadsheet uses for him.

The CSV carries the roster entry's own fields, not what the table draws: salaries as plain numbers, the raw option and rights text, the year acquired, the IR and block flags, and every spreadsheet spelling mapped onto a player. The key column is the canonical name and is what identifies a player — leave it alone if you mean to read the file back in later. "These rows" honours the filters above; the other button ignores them.
